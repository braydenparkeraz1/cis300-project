Inside the document

A WISPer plan is modeled on IRS Publication 5708, the IRS’s guide to writing a security plan for a tax and accounting practice, so anyone who knows that guide will recognize its structure.

It opens with a cover page naming your firm, the effective date, and your Qualified Individual, then an adoption page for the Qualified Individual and owner to sign, and an executive summary with an at-a-glance table of your program. Sections 1 and 2 explain why the plan exists and who runs it. Sections 3 through 8 are the working program: the data you hold, how it is protected, kept, and destroyed, who can reach it, and what happens after a breach. Section 9 keeps the plan current with testing and an annual review.

The appendices hold the details that change most often. A new laptop or a new hire means updating one table, not rewriting a section.

How the document is written

A combination of fixed language for standardized requirements and carefully reviewed AI-written sections built from your questionnaire.

Most of the plan

Static text

Fixed, standardized language for requirements that are the same for every firm, such as the Qualified Individual’s duties, training rules, disposal methods, and notice deadlines. Your answers decide which clauses apply and fill in your names, systems, vendors, and states.

“All personnel complete security awareness training at hire and at least annually, covering phishing recognition, password and MFA practices, safe document handling, and incident reporting.” From §6 · Employee Management and Training

Four passages

AI-generated sections

The executive summary overview and three passages in §3, the operations overview, the basis for each risk rating, and the risk summary, are written by AI from a fact sheet of your answers, so they describe your firm instead of a generic one. The ratings themselves are set by fixed rules, not the AI. Each passage is flagged for review, and the plan cannot be published until you approve or edit it.

Drafted from your answers about staff size, offices, the client data you hold, and the systems you use, then held for your review. Executive summary · §3 operations overview, rating notes, risk summary

When you change your answers and regenerate, paragraphs you edited carry forward. If the facts behind an edit changed, WISPer keeps your version, shows the updated wording beside it, and lets you choose. If an edited passage no longer applies, it tells you.

Alongside the plan

Two companion documents and a record of who has signed what, kept with your plan.

  • Separate report

    Security recommendations

    Best-practice improvements that the law doesn’t strictly require, such as a password manager or tighter backups, are kept out of the plan and ranked in their own report, which you can export to Word, PDF, or HTML.

  • Required every year

    Annual report to ownership

    Your Qualified Individual must report to the firm’s owners in writing each year. WISPer drafts the report from your plan’s history and the notes you add, ready to review and export.

  • Compliance records

    Signatures and reviews

    A countdown to your next review, each employee’s acknowledgment status for every version, and a DocuSign certificate of completion for each signature.

Questionnaire

You answer about twenty minutes of questions when you start, then update them whenever your firm changes. Answers save as you go, technical questions include a “Help me answer” explanation, and a review page flags blank or conflicting answers before you generate.

Sample document

The same bare-bones example shown on the home page.

Written Information Security Plan

Effective · Qualified Individual

  1. —

    Executive Summary

    An overview of your program drafted from your answers, with an at-a-glance table: Qualified Individual, next review date, overall risk, the highest-rated risks, and open items.

  2. §1

    Purpose, Scope, and Objectives

    Why adopts the plan, its authority under 16 C.F.R. Part 314, its objectives, and the terms it uses.

  3. §2

    Qualified Individual

    runs the program, reports to ownership each year, and has an optional named backup.

  4. §3

    Risk Assessment

    The client data held, where it lives, and a scored risk register: each foreseeable threat rated for likelihood and impact, with the safeguards that answer it.

  5. §4

    Safeguards — Information Systems Security

    Access control and MFA, encryption, network, devices and updates, backups, remote work, physical security, filing integrity and IRS credentials, activity monitoring, change management, application security, and AI tool use.

  6. §5

    Data Retention and Secure Disposal

    Records kept years, then destroyed: paper, drives, copier storage, and cloud data.

  7. §6

    Employee Management and Training

    Screening, a signed acknowledgment, security training with recorded completion, seasonal staff and contractors, offboarding, and enforcement.

  8. §7

    Service Provider Oversight

    Vetting, written safeguard terms, and a yearly review of every vendor that can reach client data.

  9. §8

    Incident Response Plan

    Warning signs, response contacts, who decides what, containment, and notice deadlines for the IRS, the FTC, and .

  10. §9

    Program Monitoring, Testing, and Annual Review

    A yearly control test, security testing, an IRS credential-check calendar for tax preparers, the annual written report to ownership, and a full plan review at least every 12 months.

  11. §10

    Appendices A–F

    Hardware, software, personnel and access, service providers, employee acknowledgment, and the remediation plan.
