Inside the document

A WISPer plan follows the same order as IRS Publication 5708, the IRS’s guide to writing a security plan for a tax and accounting practice, so anyone who knows that guide finds each part where they expect it.

It opens with a cover page naming your firm, the effective date, and your Qualified Individual. Sections 1 and 2 explain why the plan exists and who runs it. Sections 3 through 8 are the working program: the data you hold, how it is protected, kept, and destroyed, who can reach it, and what happens after a breach. Section 9 keeps the plan current with testing and an annual review.

The appendices hold the details that change most often. A new laptop or a new hire means updating one table, not rewriting a section.

How the document is written

A combination of fixed language for standardized requirements and carefully reviewed AI-written sections built from your questionnaire.

Most of the plan

Static text

Fixed, standardized language for requirements that are the same for every firm, such as the Qualified Individual’s duties, training rules, disposal methods, and notice deadlines. Your answers decide which clauses apply and fill in your names, systems, vendors, and states.

“All personnel complete security awareness training at hire and at least annually, covering phishing recognition, password and MFA practices, safe document handling, and incident reporting.” From §6 · Employee Management and Training

Two passages

AI-generated sections

Your operations overview and risk summary are written by AI from a fact sheet of your answers, so they describe your firm instead of a generic one. Each is flagged for review, and the plan cannot be published until you approve or edit it.

Drafted from your answers about staff size, offices, the client data you hold, and the systems you use, then held for your review. §1 operations overview · §3 risk summary

When you change your answers and regenerate, paragraphs you edited carry forward. If the facts behind an edit changed, WISPer keeps your version, shows the updated wording beside it, and lets you choose.

Questionnaire

Company-specific information is collected once, when you first use the service. It takes about twenty minutes, saves as you go, and every question has a “Help me answer” explanation.

Bare-bones example

Sample document

The same bare-bones example shown on the home page.

Written Information Security Plan

Effective · Qualified Individual

  1. §1

    Purpose, Scope, and Objectives

    Why adopts the plan, its authority under 16 C.F.R. Part 314, its objectives, and the terms it uses.

  2. §2

    Qualified Individual

    runs the program, reports to ownership each year, and has an optional named backup.

  3. §3

    Risk Assessment

    The client data held, where it lives, and eight foreseeable threats mapped to the safeguards that answer them.

  4. §4

    Safeguards: Information Systems Security

    Access control and MFA, encryption, network, devices and updates, backups, remote work, physical security, and IRS credentials.

  5. §5

    Data Retention and Secure Disposal

    Records kept years, then destroyed: paper, drives, copier storage, and cloud data.

  6. §6

    Employee Management and Training

    Screening, a signed acknowledgment, training at hire and every year, and offboarding.

  7. §7

    Service Provider Oversight

    Vetting, written safeguard terms, and a yearly review of every vendor that can reach client data.

  8. §8

    Incident Response Plan

    Warning signs, response contacts, containment, and notice deadlines for the IRS, the FTC, and .

  9. §9

    Monitoring, Testing, and Annual Review

    An IRS credential-check calendar, a yearly control test, and a full plan review at least every 12 months.

  10. §10

    Appendices A–F

    Hardware, software, personnel and access, service providers, employee acknowledgment, and the remediation plan.
