The official requirements for this document, and how the generated plan meets each one.

IRS requirements, mapped

The IRS directs tax professionals to the FTC Safeguards Rule, which treats anyone who prepares returns as a financial institution, and explains its written-plan requirement in Publication 5708. Each official element is shown next to the part of the plan that satisfies it.

Requirement How the document meets it Where
§ 314.4(a)Designate a Qualified Individual to oversee, implement, and enforce the program. Names your Qualified Individual and an optional backup, and lists every duty the role carries. §2
§ 314.4(b)Base the program on a written risk assessment. Inventories the client data you hold and where it lives, then maps eight foreseeable threats to the safeguards that answer them. §3
§ 314.4(c)Design and put in place safeguards, including access controls, encryption, multi-factor authentication, and secure disposal. Covers access and MFA, encryption, network, devices, backups, remote work, physical security, and IRS credentials, then how records are kept and destroyed. §4, §5
§ 314.4(d)Regularly test or monitor how well the safeguards work. Sets an IRS credential-check calendar and a yearly test of key controls, including a backup restore and an account review. §9
§ 314.4(e)Train staff in security awareness. Requires training at hire and every year, and tracks each employee’s signed acknowledgment of the plan. §6, App. E
§ 314.4(f)Oversee service providers. Vets every vendor that can reach client data, requires written safeguard terms, and reviews each one yearly. §7, App. D
§ 314.4(g)Evaluate and adjust the program as the business changes. Reviews the whole plan at least every 12 months, and whenever systems, staff, or threats change. §9
§ 314.4(h)Keep a written incident response plan. Lists warning signs, response contacts, containment steps, and a written review within 30 days of resolution. §8
§ 314.4(i)Have the Qualified Individual report to ownership in writing at least once a year. Makes a yearly report to the firm’s ownership one of the Qualified Individual’s stated duties. §2
§ 314.4(j)Notify the FTC of a security event involving 500 or more consumers. States the 30-day FTC deadline alongside IRS reporting steps and the notice rules for each state where your clients live. §8

Sources: 16 C.F.R. Part 314, Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information and IRS Publication 5708. The plan also draws on IRS Publications 4557 and 5293.

If you are not yet fully compliant

Most small firms have a gap or two. WISPer never blocks you from generating a plan, and it never claims a safeguard you don’t have.

Covers the gaps today

The document reflects where you stand now instead of blocking you. Each missing safeguard is written as being established, and becomes a dated commitment in Appendix F, the remediation plan.

Reminds you to update

Your compliance calendar starts emailing 30 days before the annual review and repeats every two weeks until you publish. Close a gap, update your answers, and regenerate: the commitment leaves Appendix F and your own edits carry forward.

How your data is handled

WISPer asks about your firm’s practices, never your clients’ tax data. What you do share is protected with controls modeled on SOC 2 and ISO 27001.

SOC 2

The AICPA’s Trust Services Criteria for security and confidentiality.

  • Encrypted connections, with HTTPS enforced
  • Passwords stored only as salted bcrypt hashes
  • Secure sign-in cookies that expire after 12 hours
  • Rate limits on sign-in and sign-up attempts

ISO
27001

ISO 27001

The international standard for managing information security.

  • Each firm’s records walled off; every download checked against your firm
  • Card payments handled by Stripe, so card numbers never reach WISPer
  • E-signatures handled by DocuSign under ESIGN and UETA
  • Published versions kept as fixed records

WISPer’s controls are modeled on these frameworks. WISPer has not yet completed a SOC 2 audit or an ISO 27001 certification.
