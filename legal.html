IRS requirements, mapped

The IRS directs tax professionals to the FTC Safeguards Rule, which treats anyone who prepares returns as a financial institution, and explains its written-plan requirement in Publication 5708. Each official element is shown next to the part of the plan that satisfies it. For accounting firms that don’t prepare returns, the IRS-specific parts are left out.

Requirement How the document meets it Where
§ 314.4(a)Designate a Qualified Individual to oversee, implement, and enforce the program. Names your Qualified Individual and an optional backup, and lists every duty the role carries. §2
§ 314.4(b)Base the program on a written risk assessment. Inventories the client data you hold and where it lives, then rates each foreseeable threat for likelihood and impact in a scored risk register and names the safeguards that answer it. §3
§ 314.4(c)Design and put in place safeguards, including access controls, encryption, multi-factor authentication, and secure disposal. Covers access and MFA, encryption, network, devices, backups, remote work, physical security, IRS credentials, activity monitoring, change management, and application security, then how records are kept and destroyed. §4, §5, App. A
§ 314.4(d)Regularly test or monitor how well the safeguards work. Sets a yearly test of key controls, including a backup restore and an account review, the continuous monitoring or penetration testing and vulnerability scans the Rule requires, and, for tax preparers, an IRS credential-check calendar. §9
§ 314.4(e)Train staff in security awareness. Requires security awareness training at hire and on an ongoing basis, records completion, and collects each person’s signed acknowledgment before credentials are issued. §6, App. E
§ 314.4(f)Oversee service providers. Vets every vendor that can reach client data, requires written safeguard terms, and reviews each one yearly. §7, App. D
§ 314.4(g)Evaluate and adjust the program as the business changes. Reviews the whole plan at least every 12 months, and whenever systems, staff, or threats change. §9
§ 314.4(h)Keep a written incident response plan. Lists warning signs, response contacts, containment steps, and a written review within 30 days of resolution. §8
§ 314.4(i)Have the Qualified Individual report to ownership in writing at least once a year. Makes the yearly written report a stated duty of the Qualified Individual, defines what it must cover, and WISPer drafts the report for you. §2, §9
§ 314.4(j)Notify the FTC of a notification event involving the unencrypted information of 500 or more consumers. States the 30-day FTC deadline alongside IRS reporting steps and the notice rules for each state where your clients live. §8

Sources: 16 C.F.R. Part 314, Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information and IRS Publication 5708. The plan also draws on IRS Publications 4557 and 5293.

If you are not yet fully compliant

Most small firms have a gap or two. WISPer never blocks you from generating a plan, and it never claims a safeguard you don’t have.

Covers the gaps today

The document reflects where you stand now instead of blocking you. Each missing required safeguard is written as being put in place, with a dated commitment in Appendix F, the remediation plan. Other gaps are stated honestly, rated in the risk register, and listed in a separate security recommendations report.

Reminds you to update

Your compliance calendar starts emailing 30 days before the annual review and repeats every two weeks until you publish. Close a gap, update your answers, and regenerate: the commitment leaves Appendix F and your own edits carry forward.

How your data is handled

WISPer asks about your firm’s practices, never your clients’ tax data. What you do share is protected with controls informed by SOC 2 and ISO 27001.

SOC 2

The AICPA’s Trust Services Criteria for security and confidentiality.

  • Encrypted connections, with HTTPS enforced
  • Passwords stored only as salted bcrypt hashes
  • Secure sign-in cookies that expire after 12 hours
  • Rate limits on sign-in and sign-up attempts

ISO
27001

ISO 27001

The international standard for managing information security.

  • Each firm’s records walled off; every download checked against your firm
  • Card payments handled by Stripe, so card numbers never reach WISPer
  • E-signatures handled by DocuSign under ESIGN and UETA
  • Published versions kept as fixed records
  • AI drafting sees firm-level facts only, never client data

WISPer’s controls are informed by these frameworks. WISPer has not yet completed a SOC 2 audit or an ISO 27001 certification.
